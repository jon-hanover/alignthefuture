# Vision

WORKING DRAFT. This started as a voice memo where I rambled through the core
idea behind alignthefuture.org, cleaned up and then revised after a round of
questions. The thinking is intentionally still loose. Where I've made a call,
it reads as a call. Where I haven't, it's in the open questions at the bottom.

## The need

Perhaps the biggest challenge facing the next generation is whether we manage
to align AI with human interests. Whether it serves humanity, or humanity ends
up serving it. And we can't just say that's going to be handled by some
technocrat in Washington or an AI researcher in a lab.

That's a democratic process. It's going to take all of us. Lots of people, at
lots of different levels, in lots of different positions throughout the economy
and throughout society, who understand the challenges of AI alignment and have
the skills, mindsets and knowledge to set the right policies, demand the right
research, and understand that research well enough to act on it. That's how we
create the future we actually want. That's the problem area we're addressing,
and it's the why that I'd lead with.

There's a second reason that is also very real. My suspicion is that this is
where a lot of the jobs created by AI are going to be. Alignment is the one
thing we will not want to delegate to AI, because it is the work of deciding
what AI should be doing in the first place. What guardrails do we set? How do
we know whether a system is advancing our goals or undermining them?

It strikes me as essential that we keep humans at that wheel, and my gut is we
will. So this is also about getting kids ready for the actual AI economy. That
economy will be less about using AI to accomplish knowledge work tasks, because
AI will be doing that itself, and more about the governance, oversight and
distinctly human activities that keep AI in line with human interests. Jobs
for humans aligning the work of AI to human ends, to the stated goals of a
piece of legislation, a company, or a community organization. Those jobs are
probably going to be ubiquitous: in government, in companies, in the public
square.

The economic reason matters especially from an equity perspective. This is a
way to make sure kids who might not otherwise have access to this stuff get
it.

## Why now

Especially after the Hugging Face incident, we're entering a different phase
with alignment. It's urgent.

## The program idea

Still fuzzy, but I'm imagining something like Model UN meets FIRST Robotics.
FIRST isn't the specific model, it's just a good example of what I mean:
programs where kids tinker with technology and build things with it. Kids
learn about how AI research and safety research is actually conducted today:
how the work happens, where it works well, where it's gapped. But they also
learn by doing.

Concretely, set up sandboxes where kids run simulated training sessions on
models, institute different policies, and see the trade-offs that come with
each one. Does this policy actually produce the outcome you wanted? Play those
scenarios out in a simulated global economy and society. Then layer on mock
debates around what they found.

The culminating project might be: you are now the committee in Congress that
has to write the AI alignment regulations. Debate it. Codify it. Gosh, does
that seem fun? And valuable and impactful.

## Who it's for and what they leave with

High school is where we're starting. I'm very open to middle school or early
college later.

A kid walks away with a much deeper understanding of the problem of alignment
and AI safety and what's needed to ensure it. Just as important, they walk
away with a sense of agency and competency: a belief that they can play a role
in securing an aligned future.

## Curriculum

It's both the technical layer and the governance layer. Kids need to see how
alignment research is actually done today (evals, interpretability, red
teaming, and so on) and they need the policy and governance layer on top. We'd
have to bring somebody in on the technical side to keep it honest.

## Where it lives

Open-ended for now. Summer camp, after school, in-school career and technical
education class, civics class, all still on the table. My guess is the first
pilot happens out of school, just because that's easier. The longer-term
answer depends a lot on how it's funded.

## Brand and audience

For now, assume Align the Future is the brand and the name of the
organization. The first audiences are funders and collaborators, because
that's where we start. The landing page should speak to them first.

people. Could be a formal partnership, could just be thought partnership.

## Open questions

These are the ones I still need to sit with before designing anything further.
A lot of the answers are "I don't know yet," and that's fine. They're here so
the gaps are visible.

**Simulator.** The sandbox is the heart of the idea and the hardest part to
build. What is the minimum version of it? Could the first pilot run
tabletop-style, with a real model only in the background? Or does the "learn
by doing" promise fall apart without real training runs? The Quest
conversation should inform this.

**Technical lead.** Who is the person on the technical side, and are they a
volunteer advisor, a paid curriculum partner, or a lab employee on loan? Which
of the lab routes above gets there fastest?

**Pilot.** What is the smallest real thing I could run in Denver next summer?
How many kids, how many days, roughly what would it cost, and who hosts?

**Partners.** Of Run The Future, Rithm, Quest, Playlab, History CoLab, DMNS
and the labs, which are for credibility, which for distribution, and which
for money? What is the one partnership that would make everything else easier?

**Equity.** If reaching kids who wouldn't otherwise get access is a core
reason to do this, how does recruitment work? Which schools or organizations
bring those kids in, and what does it cost to make the program free for them?

**Measurement.** Understanding and agency are the two outcomes. What would I
actually measure at the end of a pilot to show a funder either one moved?

**Role.** Am I the founder of this, the incubator who hands it off, or the
advisor who helps someone else build it? Does it live inside a client
organization, a partner organization, or on its own? I really don't know yet.

**Funders.** Who are the bigger-dollar funders beyond my current network, and
which of the two fronts (AI jobs, human agency and democracy) do they each
care about?
