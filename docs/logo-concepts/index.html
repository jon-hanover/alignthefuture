Align the Future, ten monogram directions

Every mark below merges an A and an F. The F's two arms are drawn as vectors, two futures leaving the same stem. Colors, styles and structure are deliberately far apart so the tradeoff between reading the letters and reading the arrows is visible across the set.

Upright stemThe A's right leg becomes the F stem. Strongest letter reading. Concepts 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 10.
Italic stemSame structure, leaned like the sketch. Concepts 4, 6.
Sketch geometryThe A's left leg extends into the stem and carries its own arrowhead. Strongest arrow, weakest F. Concepts 7, 9.
Concept 01 · IBM Plex Mono

Blueprint

Drafting-table treatment. Thin cyan lines on a blueprint ground with construction guides left in. Open chevron arrowheads keep the vectors reading as engineering vectors, and the tiny angle callout tells the story: two paths, two headings.

Concept 02 · Archivo Black

Bauhaus

Heavy geometric strokes, butt caps, primary yellow. The lower vector is picked out in red so the eye separates the two futures instantly. Loudest of the ten and the one that survives at favicon size best.

Concept 03 · Cormorant Garamond

Civic Seal

A round emblem for the funder-and-policy audience. Forest green ring, cream field, serifed A. The two vectors become gold compass needles, which reads as governance and direction rather than technology. Works on letterhead and a lapel pin.

Concept 04 · Orbitron

Neon Terminal

Italic monoline that leans the way your sketch leans, drawn as a phosphor display. Cyan letterforms, magenta vectors, glow and scanlines. Pitched at the teenagers in the program rather than the funders, and the colors split A/F from the arrows with no effort.

Cut Paper

Chunky rounded strokes with an offset paper shadow, like layered construction paper. Navy letters, coral and teal arrows, each future its own color. Friendly and youth-facing, and the most obviously two vectors of the whole set.

Concept 06 · Manrope

Trajectory

The alignment idea made literal. Two decision nodes on the stem, a faint fan of dotted paths behind each, and one chosen vector in color leaving each node. Ink on warm white with orange and blue. The most conceptual of the set and the one that best explains the mission without a tagline.

Concept 07 · Caveat

Marker

Your sketch, kept as a sketch. Pen on lilac notebook paper, a second pass of ink where a hand would overlap. Honest about being made by a person, which fits an organization about keeping people at the wheel. Weakest F of the ten because the stem's own arrowhead is doing the F's top-arm job.

Concept 08 · Chakra Petch

Extruded

Solid block letterforms extruded into depth, like a sign or a game piece. Indigo body, orange arrows, on a cool pale ground. The extrusion gives it physical presence for signage and swag, and the arrows read as beams pointing out of the plane.

Concept 09 · Anton

Constructivist

Red disc, black diagonals, cream wedge. Poster energy from the 1920s, when graphic design was last this confident about the future. Uses the sketch's slanted geometry because diagonals are the whole language here. Reads as an arrow first, letters second, so it is a deliberate bet.

Concept 10 · Silkscreen

Pixel

Built on a fourteen by twelve grid. Lime letters, white arrows, charcoal ground. The lower vector steps diagonally while the top runs flat, so the divergence is baked into the grid. Nods to the sandbox and simulation side of the program, and it is trivially reproducible in any medium.
