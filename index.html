Prepare the next generation to secure the future of humanity.

Whether or not AI serves human interests is the biggest question facing us.

We can't rely on the labs. Or Washington. AI alignment has to be a democratic project. And we have to start now.

We envision a program where high schoolers experience AI alignment firsthand, conduct simulated safety research, run scenarios, and discuss with their peers how to secure the future for humanity.

Thinkrobotics competition meetsSim City meetsModel UN but forAI alignment.

I. The need

Keeping AI in line with human interests is going to take lots of people, at lots of levels, in lots of positions across the economy and society. People who understand the problem, who can set the right policies, demand the right research, and read that research well enough to act on it.

Our hunch is that this is also where a lot of the durable human work will be. Deciding what AI should do is the one job we will not want to hand to AI. Jobs aligning machines to the stated goals of a law, a company, or a community are going to be everywhere.

Kids who might never otherwise get near this work deserve a real shot at it.

II. The program

Students learn how AI safety research is actually done today, then do it themselves.

They run models in a sandbox, set different policies, and watch the trade-offs play out in a simulated economy and society. They debate what they find. In the culminating project they become the committee that has to write the rules, and they write them.

A student walks away understanding the problem, with the agency to solve it.

III. Why now

We are entering a different phase with alignment. The stakes are visible to anyone paying attention, and the people who will carry this work are in high school right now.
